05 · The Music

The Theme

A rising leap, answered by a stepwise fall — the four-note idea that carries the entire work.

Introduced

Stated alone by the fortepiano in the Prologue — E minor, 136 BPM, fully exposed.

Developed

Harmonised by strings, passed through the orchestra, its rising interval eventually becoming the trance lead.

Returned

Restated exactly as in the Prologue at the close of the Coda — the same four notes, changed by everything in between.

About the Virtual Orchestra

Opus No. 1: Vienna is composed and orchestrated for a full symphonic ensemble. The orchestral performance is realised through virtual instruments and sampled orchestral libraries, shaped, arranged and integrated into a modern progressive-trance production.

No real musicians or orchestras are claimed — this document credits the composition and its virtual realisation only, unless real performers are confirmed for a future live adaptation.

01 · The Work

Artistic Note

Classical form. Electronic pulse. One continuing theme.

Opus No. 1 is not intended as a reconstruction of historical music.

It uses the language and atmosphere of Vienna’s classical tradition as the beginning of an original contemporary composition — fortepiano, string writing, thematic development, the discipline of a movement.

The past is not copied. It becomes the foundation for something new: the same theme, carried into progressive trance without losing what it was at the start.

I
Prologue

Prologue

The Theme

The work begins with a single musical idea. An intimate fortepiano introduces the central Vienna theme before the orchestra gradually surrounds it.

This is the musical DNA of the entire work. Everything that follows grows from these first notes.

II
The Awakening

The Awakening

The Pulse

The theme begins to move. Strings become more rhythmic. A distant electronic heartbeat appears.

Acoustic and electronic worlds begin to recognise each other. The orchestra does not disappear. It awakens.

III
The Final Movement

The Final Movement

The Transformation

The separation disappears. Fortepiano, strings, orchestral percussion, electronic rhythm and progressive trance now exist inside the same musical space.

The original theme reaches its largest form.

IV
Coda

Coda

The Memory

After the climax, the layers begin to disappear. The electronic heartbeat becomes distant. The orchestra withdraws. The original instrument returns.

For the first time, the theme is allowed to finish.
